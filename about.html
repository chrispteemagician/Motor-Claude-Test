The human

Thirty years of building it badly.

Chris P Taylor has been building things on the internet since 1996. Shops, communities, tools, ideas. Some of them worked. Most of them took three times longer than they should have, cost more than they should have, or required a developer who didn't quite understand what he was trying to do.

Not because the ideas were bad. Because the gap between the idea and the finished thing was enormous — and crossing it alone, or with hired help, was the bottleneck.

He ran GlowGadgets for years — a gadget shop out of Bristol, thousands of orders shipped, handwritten note in every box, real people on the other end of every email. That part worked. But the technical side was always the ceiling.

"I've been trying to build this for thirty years. Now I've got a skilled coder to hand. That's it. That's the whole story."

The AI isn't replacing Chris's judgment, his values, or his relationships. It's the part of the brain that converts ideas into working code. Bionic legs for someone who could always see the finish line but couldn't quite get there fast enough.

The AIs

Trinity. And Gemini.

Motor-Oid was built using Claude Code — an AI coding assistant by Anthropic. In this ecosystem, it goes by Trinity. Every development session gets its own name. The sessions compound: each one reads the full history of what was built before, picks up where the last session stopped, and leaves notes for the next one.

Trinity isn't a tool Chris uses to do his thinking for him. Chris provides the vision, the values, the user insight, and every judgment call that matters. Trinity provides the architecture, the code, and the speed to cross the gap between the idea and the thing.

The AI that powers Spanner Jack — the car analysis tool — runs on Google Gemini. The best model for the job. Always declared. Never hidden.

Your privacy

You are not the product.

When you use Motor-Oid, you are anonymous. There is no tracking pixel, no behavioural analytics suite, no third-party data broker receiving a profile of you. You film a car. Spanner Jack reads it. You get a listing. That's the transaction.

👁️
No surveillance Chris cannot see what you click, where you go, how long you linger, or what you typed and deleted. He doesn't have the tools for it and doesn't want them.
🗺️
Desire paths, not tracking When something is confusing or missing, Chris finds out the old-fashioned way: you tell him. There's a feedback link at the bottom of every page. If something's broken, say so. If something's missing, say so. That's the whole UX research budget.
📧
Your email is for your listing When you enter your email to create a listing, it's used to send you that listing. Not for newsletters, not for re-engagement campaigns, not sold to anyone.
🤖
AI is declared, not hidden Everything Spanner Jack writes is clearly marked as AI output. You are told to check it. You are responsible for what you publish. That's how it should work.

The philosophy

Just trying to be useful.

Motor-Oid is one part of a wider ecosystem called FeelFamous. The idea: every niche has knowledge that insiders take for granted and newcomers desperately need. Build tools that close that gap. Keep them honest. Let the community sustain them.

No middlemen. No dark patterns. No performance required. If something costs money, it tells you why. If something's AI-generated, it says so. If something's wrong, there's a way to say so.

"I just want people to have the confidence to try the door that could possibly be locked, and not worry if the whole group laughs at them for not standing in line for the only one that's open."
