Where I come from

I came from wealth. Properly wealthy — famous actors around the dinner table, movie stars in the living room. All the comfort and access that money buys. It was all there.

I didn't want it.

I wasn't naive. I didn't reject it because I didn't understand it. I rejected it because I understood it exactly. I could see the cage early. Most people spend a lifetime walking into it before they notice the bars.

"They told me money doesn't grow on trees. They told me I had to be programmed."

I was 14. I wouldn't do it. I chose cannabis. I chose community. I chose something real over something comfortable.

I've never regretted that.

Give me a clue 🎈
Imagine a bird that was handed a perfectly good cage — heated perch, regular food, a window. Most birds learn to love the cage. A few look out the window long enough to remember they have wings. Gil was 14 when he looked out the window. He hasn't stopped flying since.

The village before the village

For 35 years I've been growing cannabis and sharing it with my people. And they were never customers. That word never fit.

They were family. They still are. People you go through things with. People who show up. The kind of relationship you don't build through a transaction — you build it through trust over time, through showing up, through the plant being the common ground.

I've spent my whole adult life inside a community most people don't know exists. Not a subculture. Not a scene. A genuine network of human beings who care for each other, built around something that was supposed to be shameful and turned out to be the most honest thing in the room.

That community is why I'm still here.

What it cost

I've been to jail. Been deported. Lost everything I owned — more than once. Not once. More than once.

I'm not saying that for sympathy. I'm saying it because those things happened and I'm still here at 52, still growing, still part of the community I chose when I was a teenager. That means something.

The system took what it could. It didn't take the relationships. It didn't take the knowledge. It didn't take 35 years of understanding what this plant does for people and what people do for each other when they gather around it.

"I've survived my entire life since I was 14 off of growing — and the family I grew it for."

You can lose things and still be standing. That's not a small thing.

Where I'm going

The world is catching up. The industry is moving. Legalisation, regulation, conferences, B2B deals — all of it is happening now. There's space for people like me in the legitimate world. I know that.

But I will not join the system to do it. That's not stubbornness. That's the whole point.

Thirty-five years of operating outside the system gave me something the system cannot manufacture: genuine trust from genuine people. Real relationships. Real knowledge. Real community. None of that came from a compliance document or a business plan.

I'm looking to move forward in a way that honours what was built, not one that erases it. The plant and the people — they're the same thing to me. They always have been.

Give me a clue 🎈
Some things can't be reverse-engineered. You can build a brand-new bar on the high street, kit it out perfectly, hire great staff. But you can't buy the local where three generations of the same families have been going for forty years. That regulars' warmth — that's Gil. You don't build it from scratch. You recognise it and show up.
